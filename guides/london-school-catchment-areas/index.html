How school catchment areas really work in London

Most London schools don't have a fixed catchment boundary. They have a "furthest distance offered" figure that changes every year, and that's the figure parents actually need to understand.

The two kinds of "catchment"

When parents say "catchment", they can mean one of two different things:

Fixed designated catchment area

A small number of London community primaries — and a slightly larger number of secondaries — have a formally designated catchment area. If you live inside the boundary, you get higher priority. Most Church of England, Catholic and academies do not use this model. Boroughs vary.

Distance-based oversubscription

Most London state schools use straight-line or walking distance from home to the school's main entrance as the final tiebreaker. There is no boundary. You are inside the "catchment" if you are closer than the furthest-distance-offered child in the most recent admissions round.

The figure that matters most: the school's furthest distance offered in the previous one to three admissions rounds. Most boroughs publish this annually in their composite admissions booklet. London School Directory will surface this on each school's page once parsed from the borough booklets.

How distance is measured

Each local authority chooses its own measurement method. Three main approaches:

  • Straight-line ("crow flies") — most common, easiest to compute. Used by Wandsworth, Hammersmith and Fulham, and several others.
  • Walking route — used by Camden, Islington, and others. The official measurement may differ from Google Maps's walking route by a few hundred metres, because boroughs use their own GIS systems.
  • Pupil's home to school's main entrance — sometimes the boundary of the school's grounds is used instead. Always check the published measurement point.

The order of priority in a typical oversubscription policy

Most London state primaries use a version of this hierarchy:

  1. Children with an Education, Health and Care Plan naming the school.
  2. "Looked After" children and children previously looked after.
  3. Exceptional medical or social need (rare; high evidentiary bar).
  4. Siblings of children currently on roll.
  5. Children of staff (sometimes).
  6. Faith priority (in faith schools, often two tiers).
  7. Distance from home to school.

The crucial point: distance only matters after every higher tier is exhausted. In an oversubscribed school, the "catchment" can be tiny because most places go to siblings and EHCP/LAC children before distance is even applied.

Why "catchment" is shrinking in popular London areas

Three reasons that compound:

  • Sibling priority absorbs an increasing share of available places, year after year, in a stable family area.
  • Population mix shifts. A new development opening within the existing catchment can shrink the distance overnight.
  • School roll expansion is rare in inner London now — sites are constrained.

This means a school where the furthest-distance-offered was 700m in 2019 might be 380m in 2026. Last year's figure is a better guide than the figure from three years ago.

How to estimate your real chances

  1. Find your home's straight-line distance from the school's main entrance. Use the borough's published map or any online distance tool — Google Maps "Measure distance" works for straight line.
  2. Compare to the school's published furthest distance offered in the last three rounds (look at the trend, not just last year).
  3. Look at the school's applications per place — high oversubscription means tighter distance.
  4. Check how many places were taken by higher-tier criteria last year (siblings, EHCP, LAC, faith). The borough booklet usually breaks this out.
  5. Build a balanced list of 6 preferences — see our Reception application timeline.

Common myths

Myths to ignore:
  • "If we move closer just before applications close, we'll get in." — Boroughs typically require evidence of permanent residence; short-term rentals to game catchment are routinely challenged and places withdrawn.
  • "My child got in but moved house — they'll lose their place." — Once a place is allocated and started, a subsequent move does not remove the place.
  • "Distance is the most important factor." — Only after every higher tier. Sibling priority is typically far more decisive.
  • "All schools in the same borough use the same distance method." — They often don't. Always check the specific school.

Frequently asked

Where do I find the furthest distance offered for a specific school?

Each London borough publishes a composite admissions booklet annually, usually in September, listing every school's PAN, applications, and furthest distance offered for the previous round. London School Directory is integrating these — once parsed for a school, it appears on its page.

What if I'm just outside catchment?

Apply anyway, accept the offered place, and join the waiting list. Lists are reordered each time, so as families decline, you can move up.

Are catchment areas the same for sixth form?

No. Sixth-form admissions are typically grade-based, not distance-based. Catchment is irrelevant for most sixth-form applications.

Does private school count for sibling priority?

No. Sibling priority typically applies only to a sibling who will be on roll at the same state school at the start of the new academic year.
