Ofsted ratings explained

What the four Ofsted ratings actually mean, how often schools are inspected, what changed in the 2024 framework, and why a Good school today might be a different school tomorrow.

The four ratings

Outstanding

The school is judged to perform "exceptionally". In practice, around 17% of state schools in England hold this rating, and around 24% in London — outcomes are well above average for the pupils served, leadership is strong, and behaviour and safeguarding are at least Good.

Good

The school is providing pupils with a good education. Around 70% of London state schools are rated Good. A Good rating means the school is doing its job well; it does not imply mediocrity.

Requires Improvement

The school is not yet Good but is not failing. It will typically be re-inspected within 30 months. Requires Improvement is not a permanent label — many London schools have improved from RI to Good within 2–3 years.

Inadequate

The school is either "Serious Weaknesses" or "Special Measures". Statutory intervention follows. Inadequate is uncommon in London (typically <2% of schools at any time) and usually triggers either a leadership change or an academy conversion.

What changed in 2024

From September 2024, Ofsted paused the use of single-word overall judgements for state schools in England, following recommendations from the Big Listen consultation and the inquest into the death of Ruth Perry. Schools are now inspected on individual sub-areas (quality of education, behaviour and attitudes, personal development, leadership and management, early years, sixth-form), but the headline single-word "Outstanding/Good/RI/Inadequate" overall effectiveness rating has been suspended.

What this means in practice: if a school was last inspected before September 2024, its overall single-word rating is still its public rating until the next inspection. Schools inspected since then will instead have a series of sub-area judgements. The DfE and Ofsted are consulting on a longer-term replacement scoring system (likely a "report card") expected to come into force from September 2026 onwards.

How often is a school inspected?

Frequency depends on the previous rating:

  • Outstanding: Until recently, Outstanding schools were exempt from routine reinspection. That exemption was removed in 2020, so all Outstanding schools are being inspected on a rolling basis — many for the first time in 10+ years. Expect significant rating volatility for "long-Outstanding" schools.
  • Good: Roughly every 4 years.
  • Requires Improvement: Typically within 30 months, often with a "monitoring inspection" within 18 months.
  • Inadequate: Continuous monitoring, with formal reinspection typically within 24 months and academy conversion likely.

What an Ofsted rating doesn't tell you

An Ofsted rating is a useful but partial signal. Specifically, it doesn't tell you:

  • What the school is like today. A rating from 2018 reflects 2018.
  • Whether the school is right for your child. The same school will be ideal for some children and a poor fit for others, regardless of rating.
  • How the school handles SEND, mental health support, or specific subjects.
  • The day-to-day culture: how children speak to each other, how the school responds to bullying or to a difficult phone call from a parent.

This is why we always recommend visiting your shortlisted schools in person. The corridor and classroom impression you get on a visit is data Ofsted cannot capture.

How to read an Ofsted report properly

The headline rating gets the attention, but the full report is the more useful document. Look for:

  • Information about the school at the top — this summarises pupil numbers, intake characteristics, and any recent leadership changes. A school that has just had a new head will read differently from one with continuity.
  • "What does the school do well and what does it need to do better?" — these short bullets are blunter than the rating.
  • Quality of education section — for primary, look at reading and writing; for secondary, look at curriculum breadth.
  • Behaviour and attitudes — a Good or better here matters more for day-to-day experience than the headline.

Why London ratings cluster around Good

London schools have, on average, the strongest Ofsted profile of any English region. Approximately 90% are rated Good or Outstanding. This makes Ofsted alone a weak differentiator within London — most schools you compare will be Good. To compare similar Good schools, look at:

  • KS2 progress (primary) and Progress 8 (secondary) — see each school's listing page.
  • Applications per place — a fair proxy for community trust.
  • Year-over-year trend in results.
  • Borough context — see the borough hub pages.

Independent schools and ISI

Independent schools in England are not inspected by Ofsted. They are inspected by the Independent Schools Inspectorate (ISI), which uses its own rating framework. ISI judgements are not directly comparable to Ofsted judgements. This directory currently focuses on state schools.
